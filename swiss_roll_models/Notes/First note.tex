\begin{abstract}
% TODO: 在这里写一个 5--8 行的摘要，简要说明：
% 1. 你研究的对象：gradient descent / gradient flow 在正锥中的轨迹；
% 2. 你用的工具：Hilbert projective metric / positive cone dynamics；
% 3. 你观察到的现象：大部分训练阶段 Hilbert 距离几乎保持常数，末期突然收缩；
% 4. 你推测与 Perron--Frobenius / cone-contraction 理论的关系；
% 5. 强调这是 preliminary empirical / conceptual note。
This short note reports some preliminary observations on gradient-based learning dynamics viewed through the projective geometry of a positive cone. For simple models with a nonnegative parametrisation, the parameter trajectory remains in the interior of the cone and can be monitored using Hilbert's projective metric. Empirically, we observe that the Hilbert distance to the final parameter remains almost constant for most of training, followed by a sharp collapse near convergence. This behaviour resembles a Perron--Frobenius type contraction in projective metric. We briefly describe the setup, the empirical phenomena, and some speculative connections to nonlinear Perron--Frobenius theory and Birkhoff-type contraction results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

% TODO: 这一节建议 0.5--1 页，不要太长。

Modern gradient-based learning algorithms are typically analysed in Euclidean parameter spaces. In many settings, however, it is natural or convenient to enforce a positivity structure on the parameters, either explicitly (e.g.\ nonnegative factorisations) or implicitly (e.g.\ via nonnegative activations and reparametrisations). In such cases, the parameter trajectory lives in the interior of a positive cone, and one can ask whether a projective, rather than purely Euclidean, viewpoint is more appropriate.

Hilbert's projective metric provides a natural geometry on the projectivisation of a cone. In the context of positive linear and nonlinear operators, the Birkhoff--Bushell theory shows that order-preserving homogeneous maps often act as strict contractions in this metric, leading to Perron--Frobenius type asymptotics.

The goal of this note is to report a simple but robust empirical phenomenon: when monitoring the parameter trajectory of a gradient-based learning procedure in Hilbert's projective metric, we observe a characteristic ``plateau-and-collapse'' behaviour of the distance to the final parameter. This pattern suggests a qualitative similarity with Perron--Frobenius--type contraction on a cone. We do not attempt any rigorous analysis here, but instead outline the setup, the observations, and some possible interpretations.

\section{Setup: Gradient Dynamics in a Positive Cone}

% TODO: 这一节里给出最简但清晰的模型：

\subsection{Model and parametrisation}

Let $\cone \subset \R^n$ denote the standard positive cone
\[
  \cone := \{x \in \R^n : x_i > 0 \text{ for all } i\}.
\]
We consider a parametrised model $f_w$ with parameter $w \in \cone$.
In practice, one may obtain $w \in \cone$ either by an explicit nonnegative constraint or via a reparametrisation such as
\[
  w = \phi(\theta), \qquad \phi: \R^n \to \cone,
\]
where $\phi$ is, for instance, a componentwise softplus or exponential map.

Given a loss function $L(w)$ defined on $\cone$, we study (discrete-time) gradient descent
\begin{equation}
  w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta_t \nabla L(w_t),
  \label{eq:gd}
\end{equation}
or, in a continuous-time approximation,
\begin{equation}
  \dot{w}_t = -\nabla L(w_t).
  \label{eq:gf}
\end{equation}
For sufficiently small stepsizes and suitable regularity assumptions, the trajectory $(w_t)_t$ remains in $\cone$ for all relevant times. In what follows, we assume that $(w_t)_{t \ge 0} \subset \cone$ and that a limiting point $w^\ast \in \cone$ is reached (or very closely approximated) in the course of training.

\subsection{Projective viewpoint}

Since $\cone$ is invariant under positive scalar multiplication, it is natural to identify parameters that differ only by a positive scaling factor. That is, we consider the projective space of rays in $\cone$,
\[
  [x] = \{\alpha x : \alpha > 0\}, \qquad x \in \cone,
\]
and view the parameter trajectory in terms of its changing projective direction rather than its Euclidean location. Hilbert's projective metric provides a canonical way of quantifying the relative position of two rays.

% ---------- 3. Hilbert's Projective Metric ----------
\section{Hilbert's Projective Metric}

% TODO: 在这里给出 Hilbert 度量的定义 + 最基本性质：
% - 尺度不变；
% - 正锥内部定义；
% - 提到 Birkhoff 收缩但不用详细证明。

\begin{definition}[Hilbert projective metric]
For $x,y \in \cone$, define
\[
  M(x,y) := \max_{1 \le i \le n} \frac{x_i}{y_i}, 
  \qquad
  m(x,y) := \min_{1 \le i \le n} \frac{x_i}{y_i},
\]
and set
\[
  \Hil(x,y) := \log \frac{M(x,y)}{m(x,y)}.
\]
\end{definition}

The quantity $\Hil(x,y)$ is invariant under positive rescaling of either argument:
\[
  \Hil(\alpha x, \beta y) = \Hil(x,y) \quad \text{for all } \alpha,\beta > 0,
\]
and thus descends to a well-defined metric on the projective space of rays in $\cone$. Geometrically, $\Hil(x,y)$ measures the logarithmic spread of the coordinatewise ratios $x_i/y_i$ and can be interpreted as a notion of ``projective angle'' between the two rays.

In the theory of positive linear and nonlinear operators, an order-preserving homogeneous map $T : \cone \to \cone$ often acts as a contraction with respect to $\Hil$, with a contraction coefficient that can be bounded in terms of the projective diameter of the image of $\cone$ under $T$. This underlies many versions of Perron--Frobenius theory and the convergence of iterates of $T$ to a dominant eigenvector or eigenray.

In contrast, gradient descent updates of the form \eqref{eq:gd} are not homogeneous and are not a priori order-preserving. Nonetheless, as we describe below, the \emph{late-stage} behaviour of \eqref{eq:gd}, when monitored in the metric $\Hil(\cdot,\cdot)$, exhibits a pattern reminiscent of a Perron--Frobenius type contraction.

We illustrate the above viewpoint on a simple supervised learning task.
For concreteness, consider a feedforward neural network trained on a classification problem such as MNIST, and focus on the last-layer weight vector $w_t \in \cone \subset \R^n$. The network is trained by stochastic gradient descent with cross-entropy loss, and the last-layer parametrisation is chosen so that $w_t$ remains strictly positive.

At each optimisation step, we record $w_t$ and, after training, we estimate a ``final'' parameter $w^\ast$ (for instance, the last iterate or a running average over the last few iterates). We then compute the Hilbert distances
\[
  \Hil(w_t, w^\ast), \qquad t = 0,1,\dots,T,
\]
and, when convenient, also the stepwise ratios
\[
  r_t := \frac{\Hil(w_{t+1}, w^\ast)}{\Hil(w_t, w^\ast)}.
\]

\subsection{Plateau-and-collapse behaviour}

% TODO: 在这里用语言描述最关键的现象：
% - 中期 Hilbert 距离基本不变（ratio 接近 1）；
% - 末期突然下降；
% - 这在多次实验、不同 seed、batch size 下都很稳健。

A robust pattern across experiments is the following:
\begin{itemize}
  \item For a large portion of the training trajectory, $\Hil(w_t, w^\ast)$ remains nearly constant. The stepwise ratios $r_t$ fluctuate around $1$, indicating that there is very little projective contraction towards the final parameter.
  \item Near the end of training, there is a relatively short phase in which $\Hil(w_t, w^\ast)$ collapses rapidly. In this regime, the ratios $r_t$ tend to be noticeably smaller than $1$, signalling a sharp contraction towards the final ray $[w^\ast]$.
\end{itemize}

This ``plateau-and-collapse'' behaviour appears to be stable across different random initialisations, batch sizes, and, to some extent, model architectures. While the Euclidean distance $\norm{w_t - w^\ast}$ typically decays more smoothly, the projective distance in Hilbert's metric makes the late-stage contraction much more pronounced.

\section{Interpretation and Connections to Perron--Frobenius Theory}

% TODO: 在这一节给出你的“猜想式解释”：
% - 不是 rigorous theorem，而是 qualitative 对照：
%   * 晚期的行为似乎像一个 positive homogeneous map 的迭代；
%   * 最终方向像 PF eigenvector；
%   * Hilbert 度量下的收缩则类似 Birkhoff coefficient < 1 的情形。

The empirical findings above invite comparison with the classical picture of Perron--Frobenius theory for positive maps on cones. There, one typically studies an order-preserving, homogeneous map $T : \cone \to \cone$ whose iterates $T^k(x)$ converge projectively to a dominant eigenvector. Under suitable conditions, $T$ is a strict contraction in Hilbert's projective metric, and the projective distance to the attracting eigenray decays at an exponential rate.

In the present setting, gradient descent \eqref{eq:gd} is neither homogeneous nor obviously order-preserving. Nevertheless, the observed projective dynamics suggest the following informal picture:
\begin{itemize}
  \item For most of training, the parameter trajectory explores a region of the cone where the effective dynamics in projective space are almost stationary: the direction $[w_t]$ drifts slowly, and $\Hil(w_t, w^\ast)$ remains nearly constant.
  \item In the late stages, the dynamics enter a regime where the effective map from $w_t$ to $w_{t+1}$ behaves approximately like an order-preserving, cone-contracting transformation, leading to a rapid alignment of $[w_t]$ with the final ray $[w^\ast]$.
\end{itemize}

One speculative way to formalise this would be to regard the late-stage dynamics as governed by a family of maps $T_t$ on $\cone$ that are close, in an appropriate sense, to a limiting positive map $T$. If $T$ admits a Perron--Frobenius eigenvector and is a contraction in Hilbert's metric, then one might expect the projective component of $w_t$ to exhibit a PF-type asymptotic behaviour, while the early and middle stages reflect the non-contractive, transitory effects of the full gradient dynamics.

We leave such considerations at the level of conjectural interpretation and focus, in this note, on the empirical pattern itself.

\section{Discussion and Future Directions}

% TODO: 这一节用来和 Francesco “open questions 对话”：
% - 提出你觉得可以做 rigorous 的方向；

The observations in this note raise several natural questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Can the late-stage dynamics of gradient descent in a positive cone be approximated by an order-preserving, homogeneous (or asymptotically homogeneous) map, to which a form of Perron--Frobenius theory applies?
  \item Is it possible to identify conditions under which the gradient flow \eqref{eq:gf} induces a contraction in Hilbert's projective metric on an invariant subset of $\cone$?
  \item How robust is the observed plateau-and-collapse pattern across architectures, loss functions, and data distributions, and can it be linked to spectral properties of a suitably linearised or averaged operator?
\end{itemize}

These questions suggest a possible bridge between the geometry of optimisation trajectories in machine learning and the established theory of cone contractions and nonlinear Perron--Frobenius operators. A more systematic study of this connection, both analytically and experimentally, is left for future work.
